# Decision Log

Scope and architecture decisions of the study, each with its reason. Validation findings
and the patches they led to are in [validation_log.md](validation_log.md).

## A. Scope

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Study question | Impact of adding data-center load to **today's Spanish system** under the proposed 80% hourly matching rule and alternative procurement policies. The background fleet is frozen: there is no capacity-expansion year, and the system has not adapted to the new load |
| Fleet year | 2025 actuals (Redeia), re-checked against the live datasets (C2). A 2030 expansion case answers a different question and is out of scope |
| Geography | 15 nodes: ES 9 (8 mainland + Balearics) / FR 4 / PT 2. France's non-Iberian borders are closed with measured flows (see B) |
| Weather years | **2024 is the reference weather year** for validation and the case study: weather, fleet data and system outcomes all close, so the weather-dependent closures (measured French border flows, observed hydro inflow, the wind-bias factor) are in-sample. 2024 was a wet, high-renewable year; other weather years would bound that, with those closures as out-of-sample transfers (stage iv, C5) |
| Temporal resolution | Hourly snapshots (8,760 steps) for the study results. Quick runs average to 3 hours (`clustering.temporal.averaging: "3h"`, 2,920 steps), applied by PyPSA-Eur upstream of `composed_*.nc`. A full hourly back-cast gave dispatch within 2% of 3h (CCGT 37.6 vs 36.9 TWh) and prices within 0.5 EUR/MWh, so 3h does not drive the price-level caveat |
| Policies | Notation per [Riepin & Brown (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esr.2024.101488). `no_dc`: the baseline without the data center. `unmatched_dc`: no policy. `strict_hourly_80_ppa_only`: **the decree's Art. 9**, at least 80% of consumption from own PPAs and battery in every hour (the headline case). `hourly_80_ppa_only`: 80% hourly matching on average over the year (the 24/7 CFE score with `CFE_t = 0`). `hourly_80_grid_credit`: the same, with grid purchases credited at the iterated hourly grid CFE factor (renewables only, as in the decree). `annual_80_res`: the decree's Art. 8 alone: the contracted PPA yield (availability × capacity, whether used, sold or curtailed) equals 80% of annual consumption, an equality so the volume is a contract rather than a floor. With no sales, a data center contracts PPAs yielding ~78% of its consumption without any rule (checked with a 0% target), so annual 80% barely binds and the grid credit does not bind. Pre-built outside the matrix: other targets through the policy name (e.g. `annual_100_res`, voluntary industry practice) and the `_cfe` suffix, which counts nuclear as clean (the decree is renewable-only). Meeting Art. 9 in every hour implies the Art. 8 volume, so no separate additionality constraint is needed. The decree's surcharges act on network tariffs, which are exogenous here: discussed, not modelled. `transition_p1_60` and `transition_p1_70_p2_60`: **the revised draft's DT1** (26 Oct 2026), the floor only in tariff periods P1 (60%, to 2028) or P1 and P2 (70% and 60%, from 2029) of Circular 3/2020, other hours unconstrained. DT1 says "el 60 por ciento de la energía consumida en las horas correspondientes al periodo horario P1", which could also be read as a share of the period's total; we apply it hour by hour, as Art. 9 and the per-hour surcharge of Art. 10 do, the stricter reading. Art. 8's annual volume is not added as a separate constraint; the floors alone bring the contracted PPA yield to 77% (2026 rules) and 95% (2029 rules) of consumption |
| Sales cap | `ex_t` is electricity sold back to the grid, capped at `sum_t ex_t <= sigma · sum_t d_t`. The cap is a scenario dimension (`excess_cap` per suite, `ex{percent}` in every output path). **The study uses `sigma = 0`**: surplus is stored or curtailed. `sigma = 0.20` (the Riepin & Brown 2024 convention) is pre-built. The `no_dc` and `unmatched_dc` baselines have no surplus and are solved once, under `ex0`. Unlimited sales are not used: the data center would overbuild to sell into a background system that cannot re-equilibrate. Sales carry a cost of 0.01 EUR/MWh, so the solver never buys and sells in the same snapshot, which free accounting variables would allow; purchases stay free, so the baseline objectives remain pure system cost. The cost is negligible, and zero without sales |
| Scenario suites | Selected by `spain_cfe.suite` from `spain_cfe.suites`, each mapping PPA siting to policies: `core` (wy2024, 500 MW, the five policies with Spain-wide PPAs plus `hourly_80_ppa_only` with local PPAs, no sales) and `full` (the same at 500–6000 MW); `draft_2026_10` (wy2024, 500 MW: no policy, the decree, hourly 80% and 90% on average, the two transition floors) selected with `--configfile config/suite.draft_2026_10.yaml`) writes its table and figures under its own `output_dir` so the two drafts' results do not mix. A `no_dc` row is added per run |
| Data center | A flat load at one site hosting the full `dc_mw`: Aragón (`ES0 7AC`) by default, where the GW-scale new-build pipeline is (AWS, Microsoft, QTS/Blackstone). Any Spanish region can be chosen in `spain_cfe.dc.sites`; each listed site hosts the full `dc_mw`. Sizes 500–6000 MW: data centers hold 6.33 GW of granted transmission access, 34% of the access granted to demand ([Red Eléctrica access map via pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.es/2026/10/02/red-electrica-asigna-633-gw-de-acceso-a-centros-de-datos-el-34-de-los-1852-gw-otorgados-a-la-demanda/), Oct 2026), and more than 12 GW of access and connection rights since 2021 ([MITECO](https://www.miteco.gob.es/content/dam/miteco/es/prensa/20260827_NdP_El_Gobierno_plantea_impulsar_los_centros_de_datos_sostenibles.pdf), Aug 2026); SpainDC projects 2.6 GW installed by 2030, 1.8–2.9 GW across its scenarios ([Informe Anual 2025](https://spaindc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/SpainDC-Informe-Anual-2025_.pdf), 2026 edition). A standalone battery with optimized duration; 7% WACC. PPA candidates (onwind, solar, solar-hsat) take the **best resource class with remaining potential** at each node, either `spain_wide` (every Spanish node in the site's synchronous area; the optimizer picks) or `local_region` (the site's region only). For a mainland site, `spain_wide` excludes the Balearics, a separate system behind one 400 MW HVDC link: without this rule, the decree portfolio placed 0.77 GW of solar there (island load 290–616 MW, 38% of that output curtailed), matched to the Aragón load only through the virtual balance |

## B. Architecture

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Base model | **PyPSA-Eur v2026.09.0 as a pinned git submodule** (commit c8209014), with no upstream file modified: every study choice lives in this overlay, so a diff against vanilla PyPSA-Eur shows them all. The pin is the gitlink in the commit tree (`.gitmodules` carries only path and URL) |
| Workflow | Two steps. (1) The upstream network build runs inside the submodule with `--configfile ../config/config.spain.yaml`. (2) The study DAG (`Snakefile`) takes over from `composed_*.nc`: `prepare_network` (patch engine) → `solve_network` (data center, procurement-policy constraints, grid CFE iteration) → `summarise_network` → `make_summary` → `plot_summary` and `plot_heatmap`; the back-cast ends in `validate_network` |
| Study stages | Two year dimensions: the `{year}` wildcard is the data vintage (2024 or 2025 rows in `data/custom`), the run is the weather year. (i) raw validation (wy2024, 2024, raw), (ii) back-cast calibration (wy2024, 2024, patched), (iii) case study (wy2024, 2025, patched, data center attached), (iv) other weather years (2025, patched; open for extension, C5). The wind-bias factor is derived in (ii) and applied in (iii) as a fixed cited constant (`wind_correction.csv`) |
| Validation design | Validation **reuses the study pipeline**: the solved `no_dc` scenario on the wy2024 network is compared against Redeia actuals in one report (generation, demand, capacity factors, CO2 and intensity, flows, prices). The validated path is exactly the path the scenarios run on |
| Background corrections | `prepare_network.py` applies an ordered list of cited corrections ("patches"), each switchable in `spain_cfe.patches`; named variants (`raw`, `patched`) select sets through the `{variant}` wildcard. The rule: **build fresh, validate, correct only what the validation demands, cite every correction.** The raw variant keeps only the France boundary closure, so the raw and patched reports together show the effect of every patch |
| France boundary | **No pan-European model.** France's external borders (GB, IT, CH, DE+BE combined) are fixed to measured hourly 2024 flows (RTE eCO2mix via ODRE), as fixed generators and loads at the nearest French node. The Spanish CFE tracing covers Spain and its direct neighbours; a second hop beyond France is third-order behind a 2.8 GW border. For the validation year, measured flows beat any coarse Europe-wide model and need no second model kept consistent. Limitations: French external trade does not respond to the data center, and other weather years need a snapshot remap of the 2024 flows (C5) |
| Interconnectors | Linearized power flow (KVL) with physical impedances; thermal × `s_max_pu` line limits. Cross-border corridors are capped to published commercial NTC where validation demanded it, by per-element `s_max_pu`/`p_max_pu` scaling, with `s_nom` and impedances untouched so the flow physics is unaffected |
| Commodity prices | Dispatch-year prices are **realized market averages** for the price-setting commodities: EUA 65.24 / 73.43 EUR/tCO2 (ICAP, 2024/2025), MIBGAS PVB gas 38.08 / 35.97 EUR/MWh_th (the hub Iberian plants buy at), CIF-ARA coal 14.83 / 12.81 (IEA). Oil and biomass fuels stay on technology-data v0.15.0 (minor, not price-setting, no single benchmark). Nuclear fuel keeps the technology-data figure, but as EUR/MWh_el (7.45, i.e. 2.43 EUR/MWh_th at η 0.326): technology-data labels it per MWh_th, while its EIA source (Table 8.4) reports mills per kWh of net generation, so dividing by η would count the efficiency twice (marginal cost 27.3 instead of 11.9 EUR/MWh_el) |
| Technology costs | technology-data v0.15.0 (EUR2025), native 2025 values including lifetimes (onwind 28.5 a, solar 37.5 a, battery store 22.5 a, inverter 10 a) |
| Resource classes | `resource_classes: 5` for onwind, solar and solar-hsat: each region is split into 5 capacity-factor bins with their own profiles and potentials, and existing plants are joined to the bin where they stand. This fixes the layout bias at the config level (a single regional profile averages over all eligible land, while real plants occupy the good sites) and gives PPA candidates their granularity. The residual ERA5 wind bias is handled in validation |
| Grid CFE iteration | A fixed number of forward passes (2) with plain replacement of the hourly grid CFE factor, as in Riepin & Brown (2024): no damping, no stopping criterion. On toy networks the portfolio sits at the fixed point after the second pass; the convergence table (objective and `max_t |ΔCFE_t|` per pass) flags a scenario that would need more, e.g. a 6 GW data center. Initialized from the solved `unmatched_dc` network; HiGHS reuses the simplex basis between passes |
| Metrics | One row per scenario in `results/scenarios.csv`, with deltas against both baselines. **DC net cost** = PPA and battery annuities + PPA operating cost + grid purchases − sales revenue. Purchases and sales settle at the **hourly Spanish price** (nodal prices weighted by background load, the price validated against OMIE): Spain is one bidding zone, so a data center never sees intra-Spain nodal spreads, and nodal settlement would add a basis risk Spanish consumers do not face. Settlement is reporting only: the optimizer minimizes system cost, so dispatch and portfolios do not depend on it. The modelled price runs 10% above OMIE 2025 (71.9 vs 65.3 EUR/MWh; the 2024 back-cast: +31%, validation log §6), and purchase costs inherit that bias. **Clean coverage** = the PPA-matched share plus grid purchases credited at the solved network's hourly grid CFE factor, for every policy; `cfe_score` credits only what each policy's constraint credits, so clean coverage is the common yardstick. Spanish price and grid CFE are weighted by background load |
| Reporting boundary | **System impact is reported for Spain**, from the saved networks (`summarise_network.py`): system cost = operating cost of Spanish assets + the data center's annuities (the frozen fleet's capital cost cancels in deltas); CO2 of Spanish generators; CO2 intensity = Spanish CO2 per Spanish generation including the PPAs (storage cycling excluded); gas = fuel of gas-fired power plants (CCGT, OCGT; cogeneration runs a fixed profile and is excluded); load-weighted spot price. Whole-model figures only trace leakage: for `unmatched_dc`, ~17% of the CO2 increase and ~27 M EUR/yr of the cost land in France and Portugal. The **data center view** follows its trades across borders: attributional intensity = grid purchases at the import-traced hourly emission factor (PPA supply at zero); consequential intensity = whole-model emission change against `no_dc` per MWh consumed. Grid purchases are rebuilt from the physical balance and checked against the solved total |
| Figures | Six summary figures (`plot_summary.py`) and one heatmap per scenario (`plot_heatmap.py`), one set per run, DC size and sales cap in `results/{run}/plots/{dc_mw}mw_ex{excess}/`, as PDF (PNG on request, as in `doc/showcase`). Scenarios from no policy to the decree's every-hour rule (last), with rule-first labels and fixed Okabe-Ito colours; siting is named only where it differs from Spain-wide; the decree is highlighted; carriers use PyPSA-Eur's `tech_colors`. One editorial style (`scripts/plots.mplstyle`): Libre Franklin, no chart frame, light gridlines, the zero line as the baseline, frameless legends, units in parentheses; bars carry their value, and the system-impact panels (cost, CO2, gas, spot price) add the change in percent of the baseline under it. Each caption states what is shown and defines each quantity. The heatmaps show the hourly CFE score and attributional CO2 intensity on a time of day (UTC) × day grid with fixed colour scales (0–100%, 0–250 gCO2/kWh); their means are the clean coverage and attributional intensity. One supplementary cross-size figure (`plot_by_size.py`, written to `plots/by_size_ex{excess}/`) for suites with several DC sizes: the system-impact panels by size for no policy, hourly 80% and 90% on average and the decree, each bar labelled with its difference to no policy at that size; the 90% bars keep the family colour and are hatched, since this figure has no target tick |
| Solver | HiGHS dual simplex (open, reproducible, no licence), 2 threads per solve, set in `spain_cfe.solving.solver`; per-solver options live in `scripts/solve_network.py`. At 3h the dispatch solves in ~2 min (~4 GB); hourly scenarios need ~10 GB. This LP has tied technologies, so a solution can carry trace output on them (e.g. 19 MWh coal) |
| Environment | No own environment: everything runs in the submodule's pixi environment (manifest and lock of the pinned commit), so both workflow steps use exactly the versions PyPSA-Eur was tested with |
| Snakemake style | Rules call their scripts through `shell:` + `PYTHONPATH` and a CLI (the `script:` directive breaks on `from __future__` imports). Each rule lists its scripts and every `data/custom` file it reads as inputs, so code and data edits retrigger the DAG |
| Data policy | Every number a script uses comes from `data/custom/*.csv` with a `source_id` registered in `doc/data_sources.md` (URL, retrieval date, licence, and the sha256 of the primary file where vendored). The eCO2mix border flows ship with a rerunnable converter script; the other one-off extracts (Ember, monthly eCO2mix nuclear) are vendored. One vendored file, the Redeia monthly CO2 JSON that shapes the CHP profile, is read directly by a patch and declared as a Snakemake input |
