# Decision Log

Scope and architecture decisions for the study, agreed 2026-10-18.
Validation findings, patches and their status live in [validation_log.md](validation_log.md).

## A. Scope

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Study question | Impact of adding data-center load to **today's Spanish system** under the proposed 80% hourly matching (24/7 CFE) policy. No capacity-expansion year: the background fleet is frozen (no co-optimization world — the system has not yet adapted to the new load) |
| Fleet year | 2025/26 actuals (Redeia 2025; unrevised as of 2026-10-20, item C3). No 2030 horizon: a future-year expansion case can be added later, but it answers a different question and is out of scope here |
| Geography | 15 nodes: ES 9 (8 mainland + Balearics) / FR 4 / PT 2. France's non-Iberian borders closed with measured flows (see B) |
| Weather years | **2024 as the reference weather year** for validation and the case study: the one year where weather, fleet data and system outcomes all close, so the weather-dependent closures (measured FR border flows, observed hydro inflow, the wind-bias factor) are used in-sample. 2010, 2013, 2020 are later sensitivity runs, where those closures become out-of-sample transfers (stated caveat). 2024 was a wet, high-RES Iberian year — the sensitivities exist to bound that |
| Temporal resolution | 3-hourly **averaged** snapshots (`clustering.temporal.averaging: "3h"`, 2,920 steps), applied natively by PyPSA-Eur upstream of `composed_*.nc` — one network per run, so coarser resolutions are never built separately. Adequacy verified against a full hourly back-cast twin (2026-10-20): dispatch within 2% (CCGT 37.6 vs 36.9 TWh), prices within 0.5 EUR/MWh, so 3h is not a driver of the price-level caveat |
| Policies | Agreed set (2026-10-19), notation per Riepin & Brown (2023): `no_dc` (baseline), `unmatched_dc` (no-policy counterfactual), `annual_100_res` (volumetric matching, voluntary industry practice: the contracted PPA yield, availability × capacity whether the output is used, sold or curtailed, equals annual consumption, an equality as in Riepin & Brown (2023) so the volume is a contract rather than a floor; the data center buys the gaps from the grid and stores, sells within the cap or curtails the surplus. Counting dispatched output instead, as before 2026-10-20, turned annual matching into near-hourly matching once sales were capped at zero; in the matrix as `annual_80_res`, the decree's Art. 8 alone, since 2026-10-20, so every policy shares the 80% target; `annual_100_res` stays pre-built. With no sales, a data center contracts PPAs yielding ~78% of its consumption without any rule (checked with a 0% target), as they cost less than grid power, so annual 80% barely binds and the grid credit does not bind), `hourly_80_ppa_only` (24/7 CFE score with `CFE_t = 0`), `strict_hourly_80_ppa_only` (**the decree's Art. 9**: every hour `>= x·d_t`; the headline case), `hourly_80_grid_credit` (beyond-decree design alternative: imports credited with the iterated hourly grid CFE factor — **renewables only, nuclear not counted**, consistent with the decree; the reported `mean_grid_cfe_es` metric follows the same definition). The `_cfe` suffix (nuclear counts as clean) stays out of the matrix: the decree is renewable-only, and it is a no-op for PPA-only policies. Meeting Art. 9 in every hour implies the Art. 8 annual volume, so no separate additionality constraint is needed. Surcharge schedules are discussed, not modelled (they act on network tariffs, exogenous here). Target sweep (90/100) is pre-built via policy names but out of the current matrix |
| Excess / sales cap | `ex_t` is electricity sold back to the grid, capped annually at `sum_t ex_t <= sigma · sum_t d_t`. The cap is **a scenario dimension** (`excess_cap` per suite; `ex{percent}` in every output path): **the study focuses on `sigma = 0`** (no sales: surplus is stored or curtailed), with figures in `results/{run}/plots/ex0/`. `sigma = 0.20` (Riepin & Brown 2023 convention, surplus can be sold) is pre-built: adding it to a suite's `excess_cap` list produces the sales-allowed scenario under `ex20`. The `no_dc` and `unmatched_dc` baselines have no surplus and are solved once, under `ex0`. Unlimited sales remain a CLI escape for tests but are deliberately not used: the optimizer would overbuild at the DC node to sell surplus into a background system that cannot re-equilibrate (frozen fleet). Sales `ex_t` carry a tiny objective cost of 0.01 EUR/MWh: as free accounting variables, `im_t` and `ex_t` let the solver buy and sell in the same snapshot (reproduced with HiGHS on a toy network), which corrupts gross purchases, revenue and hourly scores. A cost on one leg suffices; Riepin & Brown (2023) priced both on physical links. Sales rather than purchases carry it, so the `no_dc` and `unmatched_dc` objectives stay pure system cost. Its total, at most ~9,000 EUR/yr at the sales cap, is negligible against policy cost deltas of 150-310 M EUR/yr |
| Scenario suites | Config-defined cross products under `spain_cfe.suites`, selected by `spain_cfe.suite`: `core` (wy2024, 500 MW, all five policies with Spain-wide PPAs plus `hourly_80_ppa_only` with local PPAs, no sales — the working set) and `full` (the same scenarios × all DC sizes × all weather years, no sales; non-2024 weather years await their stage-iv upstream builds plus a documented remap of the 2024 France border flows onto their snapshots). A `no_dc` baseline row is added automatically per run |
| Data center | Flat load at **one site hosting the full `dc_mw`**: Aragón (`ES0 7AC`) by default — the GW-scale new-build pipeline (AWS, Microsoft, QTS/Blackstone) is there. Any ES region can be chosen via `spain_cfe.dc.sites`; multiple sites each host the full `dc_mw` (no splitting). Sizes 500/1000/2000/4000/6000 MW: Red Eléctrica has granted DCs ~6.3 GW of access (~16 GW pending; MITECO >12 GW of rights; SpainDC expects 2–3 GW installed by 2030). PPA candidates (onwind, solar, solar-hsat) at the **best resource class with remaining potential**, either `spain_wide` (every ES node in the site's synchronous area, optimizer picks) or `local_region` (DC's region only). For a mainland site, `spain_wide` excludes the Balearics (2026-10-20): a separate system behind one 400 MW HVDC link, where the first core runs placed 0.77 GW of the decree portfolio's solar (island load 290–616 MW, 38% of that output curtailed), matched to the Aragón load only through the virtual balance; standalone battery with optimized duration; 7% WACC |

## B. Architecture

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Base model | **PyPSA-Eur v2026.09.0 as a pinned git submodule** (commit c8209014), zero upstream files modified. Overlay pattern, not a soft fork: anyone can diff this repo against vanilla PyPSA-Eur and see that every study choice lives in the overlay. The pin is recorded as the gitlink in the commit tree (`.gitmodules` carries only path + URL) |
| Workflow | Two steps by design. (1) The upstream network build runs *inside* the submodule with `--configfile ../config/config.spain.yaml`. (2) Our own DAG (`Snakefile`) takes over from `composed_*.nc`: `prepare_network` (patch engine) → `solve_network` (DC + 24/7 CFE constraints + grid CFE iteration) → `validate_network` / `make_summary` / `plot_summary` |
| Study stages | Four stages on one pipeline, separated by two year dimensions: the `{year}` wildcard is the **data vintage** (2024 or 2025 rows in `data/custom`), the run is the **weather year**. (i) raw validation (wy2024, 2024, raw), (ii) back-cast calibration (wy2024, 2024, patched), (iii) case study (wy2024, 2025 fleet/prices/demand/NTC, patched, DC attached), (iv) weather-year sensitivities (wy2010/13/20, 2025, planned). The wind-bias factor is derived in (ii) and applied in (iii) as a fixed cited constant (`wind_correction.csv`) |
| Validation design | Dispatch validation **reuses the study pipeline**: it is the solved `no_dc` scenario on the wy2024 network compared against Redeia actuals in one report (generation, demand, capacity factors, CO2 and intensity, flows, prices). No separate validation codebase, so the validated path is exactly the path the scenarios run on |
| Background corrections | `prepare_network.py` applies an ordered list of cited corrections ("patches"), each switchable in `spain_cfe.patches`; named `variants` (`raw`, `patched`) select sets via the `{variant}` wildcard. Philosophy: **build fresh, validate, correct only what the validation report demands, cite every correction.** The raw variant keeps only the France boundary closure, so the raw and patched validation reports together attribute the effect of every patch |
| France boundary | **No pan-European model.** France's external borders (GB, IT, CH, DE+BE combined) are fixed to measured hourly 2024 flows (RTE eCO2mix via ODRE, vendored + sha256-registered), attached as fixed generators/loads at the nearest FR node. MWh only, no clean shares: the Spanish CFE tracing covers Spain plus direct neighbours, and a neighbour's clean share is its domestic generation mix — tracing a second hop beyond France is third-order behind a 2.8 GW border into a ~95%-clean system. For the validation year, measured flows are strictly more accurate than any coarse Europe-wide model; they also avoid maintaining a second model whose assumptions must be kept permanently consistent with the main one. Stated limitations: FR external trade does not respond to the data center, and on non-2024 weather years (stage iv) the measured 2024 flows are an out-of-sample transfer that additionally needs a snapshot remap, not yet implemented |
| Interconnectors | Linearized power flow (KVL) with physical impedances everywhere; thermal × `s_max_pu` line limits. Cross-border corridors capped to published commercial NTC only where validation demanded it (see validation log): per-element `s_max_pu`/`p_max_pu` scaling in a patch, with `s_nom` and impedances untouched so the flow physics is unaffected |
| Commodity prices | Dispatch-year prices are **realized market averages** for the traded, price-setting commodities: EUA 65.24 / 73.43 EUR/tCO2 (ICAP, 2024/2025), MIBGAS PVB gas 38.08 / 35.97 EUR/MWh_th (the hub Iberian plants buy at, not TTF or scenario values), CIF-ARA coal 14.83 / 12.81 (IEA). Oil, nuclear and biomass fuels stay on technology-data v0.15.0 (minor, non-price-setting, no single benchmark). |
| Technology costs | technology-data v0.15.0 (EUR2025), native 2025 values including lifetimes (onwind 28.5 a, solar 37.5 a, battery store 22.5 a / inverter 10 a). A single cost year needs no cross-year harmonization |
| Resource classes | `resource_classes: 5` for onwind, solar and solar-hsat: each region split into 5 capacity-factor bins with own profiles and potentials, and existing plants spatially joined to the bin where they stand. This fixes the layout bias (a single regional profile averages over all eligible land, while real plants occupy the good sites) at the config level, before any correction factor, and gives DC PPA candidates their contracting granularity (best bin with remaining potential). The residual ERA5 wind bias is handled in validation (see validation log) |
| Grid CFE iteration | **Fixed number of forward passes** (default 2) with plain replacement of the hourly grid CFE factor, following Riepin & Brown (2023): no damping, no stopping criterion. One forward pass converges well (toy traces: the portfolio sits on the fixed point after pass 2 while the reported Δ trails); the per-pass objective and `max_t |ΔCFE_t|` are written to the convergence table as the signal that a scenario (e.g. a 6 GW DC) might need more passes. Initialized from the solved `unmatched_dc` network (better than zeros); HiGHS simplex basis warm-start kept for speed |
| Metrics | One row per scenario, collected with baseline deltas into `results/scenarios.csv`. **DC net cost** = PPA and battery annuities + PPA operating cost + grid purchases − sales revenue, with purchases `im_t` and sales `ex_t` settled at the **hourly Spanish price** (nodal prices weighted by background load: the price validated against OMIE). Spain is one bidding zone, so a real data center never sees the model's intra-Spain nodal spreads, and a PPA's location does not move its settlement. Riepin & Brown (2023) settled at the DC node, which was the zonal price there (one node per country); full nodal settlement (load at its node, each PPA at its own) is rejected because it adds a basis risk Spanish consumers do not face. Settlement is reporting only: the optimizer minimizes system cost, so dispatch, portfolios and objective deltas do not depend on it. Purchases and revenue inherit the back-cast price bias (+33%, validation log §6). **Clean coverage** = the PPA-matched share plus grid purchases credited with the solved network's own hourly grid CFE factor, computed for every policy; `cfe_score` credits only what the policy constraint credits (zero grid credit for `unmatched_dc` and PPA-only policies), so clean coverage is the yardstick that compares all rows. Spanish price and grid CFE are weighted by background load |
| Reporting boundary | **System impact is reported for Spain** (agreed 2026-10-20), computed after the solve from the saved networks (`summarise_network.py`): system cost = operating cost of Spanish assets + the data center's annuities (the frozen fleet's capital cost is constant and cancels in deltas); CO2 of Spanish generators; CO2 intensity = Spanish CO2 / Spanish generation including the DC's PPAs (storage cycling excluded); gas = fuel of gas-fired power plants (CCGT, OCGT; cogeneration runs a fixed profile and is excluded); load-weighted spot price. The whole-model objective and emissions serve only as a leakage tracer: for `unmatched_dc` ~7% of the CO2 increase and ~24 M EUR/yr of the cost land in France and Portugal. The **data center perspective** follows its trades across borders: attributional intensity = grid purchases at the import-traced hourly grid emission factor (same tracing as the grid CFE factor; PPA supply at zero); consequential intensity = whole-model emission change vs `no_dc` per MWh consumed. Grid purchases are rebuilt from the physical balance, exact under the sales cost, and checked against the solved total |
| Figures | Six summary figures (`plot_summary.py`): clean coverage, PPA portfolio, DC cost, Spanish system impact, Spanish supply shift, DC CO2 intensity. Scenarios in order of relevance with rule-first labels (no policy; decree: 80% every hour; hourly 80% on average; hourly 80% average + grid credit; hourly 80% average, local; annual 100%) and fixed Okabe-Ito colours; siting is named only where it differs from Spain-wide, and every caption states it; the decree highlighted in every figure; carriers in PyPSA-Eur's `tech_colors`; one shared editorial style file (`scripts/plots.mplstyle`: Libre Franklin typeface, no chart frame, light gridlines, zero line as the baseline); frameless multi-column legends (no names written on bars), zero baselines, no dual axes for different quantities (the system-impact panels repeat their values as percent of the baseline level on the right); an in-figure caption states what is shown and the key mechanics, with numbers read from the summary table; units in parentheses; PDF output. Per policy, a heatmap (`plot_heatmap.py`) shows the CFE score and attributional CO2 intensity of DC consumption per snapshot on a time of day × day grid, on fixed colour scales (0–100%, 0–250 gCO2/kWh) so policies compare directly; the hourly series come from `summarise_network.py` and average to clean coverage and attributional intensity. The figures cover single-run suites and fail fast otherwise: the full suite gets its own designs (DC size on the x-axis, weather years as ranges) |
| Solver | HiGHS dual simplex as the documented default (open, reproducible, no license). Gurobi supported via `spain_cfe.solving.solver` with **plain solver defaults** — benchmarked on the full-year dispatch: HiGHS 119 s, Gurobi 25 s, identical objectives, ~4 GB peak RAM either way. The pypsa-eur `gurobi-default` barrier settings (crossover 0) were tested and rejected for this study: marginally slower on our small LP and an interior-point solution smears duals on degenerate ties. Grid-credit basis warm-start applies to HiGHS only |
| Environment | No own environment: everything runs in the submodule's pixi environment (manifest + lock shipped with the pinned commit), so both workflow steps use exactly the versions the PyPSA-Eur release was tested with and there is no second manifest to keep in sync. An own minimal `pixi.toml` is added only when the study needs a package PyPSA-Eur lacks |
| Snakemake style | All rules invoke scripts via `shell:` + `PYTHONPATH` and a CLI (the `script:` directive breaks on `from __future__` imports because its wrapper prepends code). Every `data/custom` CSV a script reads is declared as a rule input so data edits retrigger the DAG |
| Data policy | Every number a script uses comes from `data/custom/*.csv` with a `source_id` registered in `doc/data_sources.md` (URL, retrieval date, licence, sha256 of the vendored primary file). The eCO2mix border flows ship with a rerunnable converter script; the other one-off extracts (Ember, monthly eCO2mix nuclear) are vendored with their API query documented in the registry. Two vendored series (the Redeia monthly CHP JSON, the eCO2mix nuclear extract) are read directly by patches and are declared Snakemake inputs and registered like every CSV |
